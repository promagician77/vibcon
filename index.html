The fix, and why it belongs in Phase 1

  ARM    = civil dusk  (sun crosses -6 deg, descending)  + site offset
  DISARM = civil dawn  (sun crosses -6 deg, ascending)

Derived per day from latitude and longitude, so it is correct at any site you add without anyone editing a schedule. Norway also runs CET and CEST, so "18:00 local" is two different solar moments depending on the month, and the error above is not even stable across the year.

This is Phase 3 and you are funding Phase 1. I raise it now because it is a schema decision: the site table has to carry coordinates from day one. That costs nothing now and is a migration later.

Your requirement 1 and your requirement 2 pull against each other

1. "...handle extreme, concurrent time-series ingestion strings without truncation or performance lag."

2. "...the 5 core telemetry compliance channels: mm/s, m/s², Hz, full Fast Fourier Transform arrays, and peak amplitudes, including raw time-series waveform files."

Requirement 2 puts two completely different data shapes in one list. Requirement 1 then rules out what happens when you store them together.

against 12 GB of compressed telemetry. The bulk data is forty times the size of the thing the database is good at. These are sizing assumptions, not measurements of your system. I do not know your sample rate, event length or sensor count, and those three answers change every number above.

What that does inside PostgreSQL. Any value over about 2 kB is moved out of line into a TOAST table. A 1.47 MB waveform becomes several hundred TOAST chunks in a side relation. TimescaleDB compression cannot reach them. Vacuum, backup and restore all carry the bulk even for queries that never select a waveform. Chunk pruning stops helping. A 160 GB Droplet holds about three months of it and nothing else.

The split

  waveform_sha256   bytea(32)   <- the object key AND the integrity record
  waveform_bytes    integer
  waveform_uri      text        <- s3://vibcon-waveforms/<sha256>.f32
  sample_rate_hz    integer
Your Phase 2 SHA-256 requirement comes out of this for free. You asked for records "stored immutably using a SHA-256 validation hash string". Store the file under its own digest and the hash is not metadata bolted on afterwards, it is the address. A file that does not hash to its own key is corrupt or altered, and anyone can check that without trusting me. Deduplication is free as well, and object storage is roughly an order of magnitude cheaper per GB than block storage on a Droplet.

One caveat on "immutably". A SHA-256 makes tampering detectable, not impossible. Whoever can rewrite the row can recompute the hash. If these records might one day have to be defended, the digests need to be append-only and anchored where the same party cannot silently rewrite them: an object-lock bucket, a signed digest-of-digests, or an external timestamp. That is a Phase 2 conversation, but the schema either leaves room for it or it does not.

Two things to settle before Phase 3 and Phase 4 are scoped

The EYE does not speak LoRaWAN

"Integrate wireless MQTT/LoRaWAN gateway inputs from White-Label VibCon Sensors (Teltonika EYE hardware)"

The Teltonika EYE (BTSMP1) is a Bluetooth Low Energy device: iBeacon and Eddystone plus a proprietary mode, temperature, humidity, accelerometer, magnet detect, about 80 m range, multi-year coin cell. There is no LoRaWAN radio in it, and LoRaWAN is not a firmware option on a BLE part.

  EYE (BLE) --> BLE gateway --> MQTT over the gateway's backhaul --> broker
                every ~80 m line of sight, less across plant and rock faces

  LoRaWAN would be kilometres from one gateway, but needs a different sensor.

That is a capital decision, not a software one: the radio decides how many gateways you buy. It should be settled before Phase 3 is scoped rather than during it.

And a question I am not going to answer for you

Phase 1 wants mm/s, a dominant frequency, a full FFT and raw waveforms. That is seismograph-class instrumentation: a tri-axial geophone sampling in the kilohertz range with a triggered recorder. The EYE's accelerometer is a movement sensor on a beacon, which is right for "has this gate moved" and wrong for "what was the peak particle velocity at the foundation during that blast".

Are the Phase 1 vibration sensors and the Phase 3 perimeter sensors the same hardware? If they are, Phase 1 cannot produce the five channels it asks for, and that is worth knowing before the milestone is funded. NS 8141: which edition?

Phase 4 wants the NS 8141 formula automated. Before a line of that is written, one thing has to be recorded in the system: which edition. The public record disagrees with itself, so I am not going to pick the confident-sounding answer.

SourceSays
Norwegian Tunnelling Society presentationPart 1 (blasting) was withdrawn in 2016 after objections to its limits and frequency filter; NS 8141:2001 used afterwards
One standards retailerNS 8141:2001 listed as withdrawn
Another standards retailerNS 8141-1:2022 listed as current
NGV 2022 conferenceA revision that removes the frequency filter and simplifies adjustment factors
The frequency filter is the point. If one edition applies a frequency-dependent correction and another does not, the same waveform yields a different limit under each. Hard-code one formula and the system silently produces the wrong verdict the moment the standard moves, for every historical record as well. Standard Norge is the authority and your consultants already know which edition they work to. One sentence from you settles it.
  limit_rule   standard, edition, effective_from/to, parameters jsonb, source_note
  assessment   event, limit_rule_id, measured_ppv, computed_limit, verdict

Every assessment records which rule judged it. An edition change adds a row rather than a deploy, and a record from 2027 is still explainable in 2031. Same discipline as your Phase 2 hashing, applied to the rules instead of the data.

One measurement note. NS 8141's limits apply at the foundation of the structure being protected, not at the quarry face and not wherever a sensor happened to be mounted. So the schema records what each sensor is bolted to, otherwise a compliance report measures the right quantity in the wrong place.
